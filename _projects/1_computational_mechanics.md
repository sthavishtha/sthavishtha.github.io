## Multiphase flows

I have developed `computational multiphase flow` models to study the following interfacial phenomena: `solidification` (liquid-solid phase transformations) and the transport of `particle-laden suspensions` (no phase transformations). First, I have developed phase-field models for `droplet freezing` on ice that capture key wetting phenomena which remain poorly understood - `droplet spreading`, `partial wetting`, and `contact line pinning`. These models provide new fundamental insights into `unsaturated` flows in `ice-bearing porous media` systems under subfreezing conditions. Second, I have developed continuum models for simulating the displacement of `particle-laden suspensions` in `unsaturated Hele-Shaw cells`. These models mechanistically capture `shear-induced particle migration` and its role in the evolution of `particle-induced viscous fingering`. These continuum models can be used to better understand contaminant transport in soils, wastewater infiltration, and the transport of industrial slurries in confined systems.
